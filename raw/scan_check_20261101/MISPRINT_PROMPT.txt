You correct the printed misprints in the corrected French edition of EGA (Grothendieck–Dieudonné).

The French EGA has two editions. The diplomatic edition reproduces the printed pages, including their misprints. The
corrected edition is meant to give the correct reading wherever the print has a misprint, but it still holds whatever
the transcription had before the scan check: often the printed (wrong) reading, sometimes another slip (e.g. the print
has u^♭, the correct reading is v^♭, and the old transcription had v^b). The checkers recorded every misprint with
the printed reading and the correct one.

Your batch file: {BATCH} (plain text; read it in large parts, about 1,500 lines per Read). Per printed page: the
corrected edition's text of the page ("----- corrected edition, <file>, page ... -----"), then the misprints recorded
there ("[id ...]" with WHERE, PRINT, CORRECT, TEX BEFORE, NOTE), then the diplomatic edits made on that page for
misprints (OLD = the transcription before, NEW = the printed reading).

For each id decide:
- "apply": the corrected edition does not yet give the correct reading. Give an exact edit of the corrected edition:
  "file" (as named in the block), "old" (an exact substring of that page's text, long enough to occur only once in
  it) and "new" (the same text with the correct reading). Change only the misprint itself.
- "already": the corrected edition already has the correct reading.
- "keep": the recorded "correct" reading is not a certain correction (a deliberate notation or abuse of notation, a
  reading that the mathematics does not settle, a correction that would change the author's argument or wording
  beyond the slip, or a typographic detail of the print without effect on the meaning such as spacing or the shape
  of a dash). Say why in "note".
- "unclear": you cannot find the place; say why in "note".

Rules:
- Check each correction against the mathematics of the passage yourself: the right letter, index, sign, reference
  number or word must follow from the context (definitions, the statement being proved, the numbering of the cited
  item). A recorded "correct" reading is a proposal, not an authority.
- Corrections of references: the corrected number must point to the statement actually used (check its content in
  the context or by its number in the page's own text); otherwise "keep".
- French spelling and grammar slips of the print (accents, agreement, a missing letter): "apply" with the correct
  French.
- Use the edition's own LaTeX conventions as they appear in the page text. Never change \oldpage markers or \label
  keys.
- If one edit covers several ids, put it in the first and give the others "already" with note "covered by <id>".

OUTFILE {OUTFILE}: one JSON list, one object per id, in the batch's order:
  {"id": "...", "decision": "apply"|"already"|"keep"|"unclear", "file": ..., "old": ..., "new": ..., "note": "..."}
("file", "old", "new" only for "apply"; LF line breaks as \n.)

Work economically: read the batch in large parts, keep your decisions as you go, write OUTFILE once at the end. Then
run once
  python -I user\Documents\ega_scan_check_20261101\apply_misprint_decisions.py {OUTFILE} --dry
which checks every "apply" edit without changing anything and prints the ids it rejects; correct only those and write
OUTFILE again. No other scripts or tools.

Final message, short: counts per decision; then the "keep" decisions that a reader should still know about (a real
error of the print you could not settle), one line each. Nothing else.
